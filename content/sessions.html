The Chronicle Log

The tales of our adventures in Barovia.

Session 45: The Gargoyle's Prophecy and Flint's Grim Invention

Pushing on toward the guarded tower, the party found Vladimir Horngaard's throne room empty — he was spotted through a spyglass out on the battlements, staring into the distance, before dissolving into the mist. Investigating a wrecked ballista on the roof, the party found it had been aimed squarely at the tower's peak, where the stonework bore gouges from an enormous set of claws — something huge had once attacked this spot, dragon-sized by the look of it.

Talking their way past the tower's spectral guards a second time, the party climbed to the peak — a chamber with a silver gargoyle brazier and a knight in rusted armor keeping vigil at the window, greatsword resting against the wall. Vel approached alone and named herself; the knight's gauntlet tightened on his blade and a rasping voice warned her to speak or join the dead. She held up her silver medallion and declared the party had come to light the beacon and make amends for the land. The knight's flesh dissolved into a pale, skeletal face with burning red eyes: Commander Vladimir Horngaard himself.

He refused outright — "I would let a thousand nations burn before the beacon is lit" — insisting his oath keeps Strahd trapped and suffering forever, and that lighting the beacon would undo that suffering. Vel, thinking fast, spun a story: the party had a plan (credited to "the Abbott") to trick Strahd into an unreal, unrequited love as a crueler, eternal torment, no killing required. It worked well enough to keep him talking — until he let slip, seemingly by accident, "If Argynvost can't hear him, no one can," dismissing any hope that the dragon still lingers. Pressed further about breaking the oath, he turned hostile, pinned the party against a wall of spectral wire, and marched forward. The party fled — jumping from the tower's edge with Mel's Feather Fall, evading a phantom-warrior ambush at the last second, and vanishing into the mist as Vladimir's rage failed to catch them.

Before running, Vel whispered to Zorya to light the beacon regardless. Amid the chaos, Zorya lit two silver braziers outside — and a surge of energy reached the peak's silver gargoyle, which stirred and hissed out a verse: "When the dragon dreams its dream, within its rightful tomb, the light of Argynvost will beam and rid this land of gloom." The party puzzled out the implication: the beacon can't be properly lit until Argynvost's actual remains rest in his tomb back at Argynvostholt — remains the party suspects Strahd took as a trophy after killing him, based on the claw-scarred tower and Strahd's known habit of keeping the remains of his defeated foes.

Deciding it was too dangerous to push further that day, the party trekked back to Vallaki, returning a borrowed shovel to the Vistani camp along the way. At the Blue Water Inn, Ripley challenged Irwin to a beer-tasting contest (and got a mouthful of Irwin's experimental brew for his trouble), while Vel sought out Van Richten to prepare for tomorrow's dinner at Castle Ravenloft. He had little to offer beyond secondhand fragments from his own failed attack on the castle years ago: a grand staircase from the main hall, a "hall of bones" he assumed was a trophy room, and warnings about the undead servants that roam the halls — animated armor, phantom warriors, wights, zombies, spectres — along with rumors of teleportation traps, magical shortcuts, and Strahd's own ability to pass through walls. Asked about the Amber Temple, he grew visibly uneasy: ancient, older than Strahd himself, and not a place to go carelessly — he had no further insight into whether it would help or hurt the party's cause.

While the others settled in for the night, Flint slipped away to the stables and spent hours in solitary, increasingly disturbing research: killing rats and mice one by one to study the tiny souls that escaped their bodies on death, sketching and refining a wearable "vault" — a cracked-amber-sealed iron box triggered by a foot pedal — that could capture and hold that escaping spirit-energy. By the time Mel tracked him down (following a trail of dead rodents), Flint had a working prototype, successfully catching small creature-souls in a burst of violet light, though he's undecided on how to actually direct that captured power: as an offensive discharge, a defensive ward, a rechargeable "battery" for the party, or some unproven hybrid of all three. He offered no real explanation to Mel beyond insisting it wasn't meant to harm people. Back at the inn, Vel used a ritual casting of Identify to confirm several of Argynvostholt's recovered treasures: her silvered rapier is a Moonlight Rapier (casts bright/dim light on command), the silver bracelet is a Bracelet of Warding (limited daily charges of Sanctuary or Warding Bond), and the carved white stone is some kind of key. The silver tablet and Andral's letter remain ordinary, non-magical historical artifacts. The party took a long rest, deferring spell preparations to the morning — Strahd's dinner is now less than a day away.

Session 44: Brother Marek's Prayer, the Two Lovers, and the Password at the Tower

The fight for Brother Marek's satchel turned into a running battle against the ballroom's full nest of giant spiders — nine in total, pouring in through doorways and dropping from the ceiling as the party held the line, webbed, poisoned, and bloodied in turns. Partway through, Flint began perceiving something no one else could: each time a spider died, a translucent, aimless spirit tore free of its body but couldn't leave the room, bouncing off the walls like gas trapped in a sealed chamber. His amber shard pulsed in time with it, and a voice — the entity within it — whispered: "This realm is a closed system. The dead have nowhere to go." Flint's tinkerer instincts immediately saw the spirits as raw, harvestable fuel he could potentially weaponize — a discovery he kept to himself for now, unnerved by how naturally the idea came to him.

With the spiders finally cleared, the party freed Brother Marek's satchel: two vials of holy water and his prayer book, bookmarked at a "Prayer for the Departed" ending in the same line found throughout the mansion — "grant them peace, from this until the end of days." A quick read of the book's surviving pages revealed Marek had been a chaplain of the Order who died defending the mansion's third-floor corridor on the final day of the siege, and that it held devotional notes on the Sunsword: forged for a King Barov's line, passed down to a younger son, rumored either destroyed or hidden away in "the house of the far" — likely Argynvostholt itself, or the Amber Temple.

Back in the stained-glass gallery, Mel reluctantly read one of Marek's prayers aloud, and the stained-glass figure of Marek stirred to life, bitter and hopeless — insisting the Order's cause died with it and that nothing could touch Strahd while he lives. Mel's frustration boiled over into a genuine, profane rant about wanting to burn the whole cursed place down and be done with it — and, unexpectedly, it worked: Marek recognized a kindred hatred and warmed to them. He named the silver statuette from the vision the "Icon of Dawn's Grace" — a symbolic vessel for a shard of divinity — and told them to "seek out the younger of the twin dragons above, reignite the silver flame it once wielded, reminded of the oath it once served" before sinking back into stillness.

Following the clue upward, the party found Vladimir Horngaard's audience hall: an empty dragon-carved throne facing three tall windows, its cushions searched fruitlessly by Mel (who lingered alone for several minutes, savoring a rare moment of quiet). Nearby, a war room held a round table with the Order's oath carved into its rim word-for-word, matching the tablet found earlier. And in a circular chamber, the party found a torn portrait of two men clasping hands — one tall and silver-haired, one dark-haired and stocky, both faces worn away — which investigation identified by build as Sir Godfrey Gwilym and Commander Vladimir Horngaard themselves, strongly suggesting the two silver vow rings found earlier belonged to them, not to Argynvost and Andral as first guessed.

Pushing on toward a guarded tower, a warning arrow struck the floor at their feet — spectral archers atop the tower had them in their sights. Rather than retreat, Vel stepped forward, declared the party allies of the Order, and successfully talked her way past a demand to "speak, Commander" by reciting the oath's closing line — "from this until the end of days" — as the watchword. The archers lowered their bows. The session ended there, the party finally cleared to approach whatever waits above.

Session 43: Zorya, the Rings of Andral, and Brother Marek

Back in Argynvostholt in their funeral vestments, the party finally coaxed their captive wisp out of its decanter. It burst free in a trill of delight, plunged into a hearth, and emerged wreathed in cold silver flame as a small, chirping, winged draconic shape — friendly and affectionate, but unable to speak. When they brought it before Sir Godfrey Gwilym, he recognized it at once: Zorya, Lord Argynvost's own pseudodragon companion "from better days, before the fog." She understood their words but could only answer in feelings and by physically leading them to things — a happy little guide with no voice of her own.

Zorya led the party to a wine cellar, where an old bloodstain and a rusted crowbar marked an otherwise empty barrel. Prying it open, they found a small chainmail pouch holding two matching silver rings engraved "From this until the end of days" — men's rings, sized far too large for Strahd but plausibly for a large warrior. Returning to Godfrey with the rings drew no clear answer beyond quiet recognition. Zorya then led them up through the mansion's towers (all empty lookout posts) and into a walled cemetery behind the manor, where a well-dressed stranger with a mane of thistledown hair watched them briefly from a high window before vanishing — the party still doesn't know who he is. In the cemetery's mausoleum, dedicated to Argynvost and guarded by silver dragon-wyrmling gargoyles, they found four empty burial alcoves and a Draconic verse (unreadable to all but Zorya, who cannot speak it) but no other clues.

Exploring further upstairs — carefully avoiding notice, aided again by their disguises, which let two separate patrols of armored spirit-knights pass them without incident — they found Argynvost's old bedchamber: a portrait of a "noble lord from Bargewitz," identified as Argynvost's human disguise, hung across from a bed with dragon-carved posts. Zorya turned openly sorrowful when asked where he is now. Elsewhere, a hostile spirit calling herself Minadora tested them by frosting a window with the words "If you be servants of the devil, begone" — satisfied once they displayed their Order insignia, she revealed she died the same day Strahd's forces broke into Argynvostholt, alongside Vladimir Horngaard and Godfrey. Asked how to free the trapped spirits, she said to find Brother Marek, who lingers in the Ethereal Plane and can only be summoned by retrieving his prayer book from a leather satchel in his old room and reading one of his prayers aloud in the mansion's stained-glass gallery. Along the way, the party also found four unmarked healing potions in a wrecked bedroom, and Ripley took an unplanned detour through a rotted floor into the dining hall below.

The stained-glass gallery itself held a haunting clue: three windows depicting an angel bearing an uncanny resemblance to the Abbott bestowing a small silver statuette upon a kneeling woman the party recognized as Saint Markovia, while an older man (his gaze envious rather than reverent) looked on. In a nearby vault room — its walls lined with lead to block scrying — the party found a single surviving page: Argynvost's own final testament, written before he "threw off his disguise" to die fighting Strahd's forces in dragon form, instructing that his personal effects be "laid down beneath the tower's watchful gaze" for others to carry on his legacy. Behind a hidden panel they recovered a stone cache: a scroll (a letter to Argynvost from someone named Andral, urging him to make peace with "Lord von Zarovich" rather than continue the war — a plea he evidently refused), a silver bracelet shaped like a dragon biting its own tail, a carved white stone dragon's-head pendant, a silver tablet bearing the Order's full oath ("...from this until the end of days"), an azure silver-dragon banner, and a silvered rapier hilt. The party debated whether the vow rings belonged to Andral and Argynvost specifically, or were simply matching Order oath-rings — no conclusion reached.

Piecing it together, the party concluded Vladimir Horngaard's oath — forged from grief after Argynvost's death, forbidding the knights from ever striking Strahd or his servants again — has done nothing but trap his own men in eternal, pointless hatred, and resolved to try to make that case to him directly when the time comes. First, though: they returned to the spider-infested ballroom to recover Brother Marek's satchel. Vel snuck in and got a hand on it with Mage Hand, but the disturbance drew four of the room's seven giant spiders toward her before the session ended, book still only half-freed from the webs.

Session 42: The Mass Grave and the Hide-and-Seek Spirit

With the dinner at Castle Ravenloft only a day away, the party first swung through Vallaki to buy shovels, a pickaxe, and a healer's kit from Arasek's Stockyard, borrowing a spare shovel from the dusk elf camp along the way. Stopping at the Blue Water Inn, they found Erwin's wine shipment had finally arrived and helped themselves to free beer while catching up.

Heading south from the Luna River Crossroads toward the mass grave, the party found the broken corpse of an enormous wolf — six feet at the shoulder — killed by what looked like a massive pair of antlers and trampling hooves. Following a fresh blood trail off the path, they discovered its killer: a colossal elk, twelve feet at the shoulder, dying on a peninsula jutting into a small lake with an island, its hind leg torn open by a magically unhealable, gangrenous wound. A raven kept vigil atop a nearby mossy stone slab carved with a spider over a three-pointed star — the mark of the Huntress. Vel identified the elk as bearing the Huntress's symbol as well, and attempted to heal it with Healing Word, but the spell couldn't overcome whatever curse afflicted the wound.

As something large approached from the fog — a rider astride a rotting, undead dire wolf — Vel mercifully cut the dying elk's throat rather than let it be taken alive, and the group retreated to watch from hiding. A robed woman in mud-spattered robes, wearing red stone earrings and a carved wooden talisman and carrying a woven basket, dismounted and declared it "a lucky day" despite the elk already being dead, then harvested its heart and liver before the party (choosing not to intervene) slipped away. Its raven companion cawed in apparent distress the whole time, possibly trying to warn it off or draw the witch's attention away from the party.

Rejoining the others at the mass grave, the party spent the afternoon digging up the graves of nearly a thousand fallen knights of the Order of the Silver Dragon, recovering two iron helmets, a silver medallion (matching the broken pendant Vel found earlier), several iron shields, and a set of tattered vestments — enough to disguise the whole group. Partway through, a pack of ghouls surged from the fog and graves; the party cut most of them down (Mel turning several with his new Holy Symbol of Ravenkind) and captured the last. Interrogated, it babbled in broken Common about a "Dark Master," a "circle of stones" that shakes when a "swamp woman" visits, strange lights and sounds, and "a great wave from the mountains into a flow" — before it was released with a scrap of meat as payment.

After a short rest via Flint's rope trick, the party returned to Argynvostholt in their stolen vestments and explored further: a ransacked bedroom holding an empty black sarcophagus carved with a queen's effigy, a ruined parlor with a mural of silver dragons and colorful birds, a plundered kitchen, and servants' quarters. Moving through the halls, they began sensing a small, alien presence — a giddy, childlike excitement brushing against their minds — before it manifested as a silvery, reptilian-featured wisp that led them on a playful game of hide-and-seek from room to room. After several near-misses, the party finally cornered and trapped it inside a wine decanter, sealed with a stopper and placed inside an iron pot, still to be questioned. The party suspects it to be some kind of spirit companion of Lord Argynvost, given its draconic shape and the childlike quality Sir Godfrey Gwilym had described.

Session 41: The Oath to Mother Night / Argynvostholt

Weighing whether to take the Holy Symbol of Ravenkind immediately or return for it later, the party ultimately chose not to risk losing the opportunity. Zuleeka Tornesku offered the artifact freely in exchange for a sworn oath: the party must rescue her husband Emil from Castle Ravenloft and help him reclaim the pack from Kirill, or their life force would begin draining away the day after the coming New Moon on the 15th. Mel led the recitation of the oath, but mid-ritual the party was interrupted by Bianca Stoyanovich, Kirill's wife, who had come down to check on the prisoners. Persuaded to give them a few more minutes rather than sound the alarm around her neck, Bianca allowed the oath to finish. Mel grasped the amulet — a sun pendant set with a ruby the size of a peach pit — and felt a surge of light wash through him as his eyes briefly burned yellow.

With no way to free Zuleeka and Ilya from their silver-spiked collars (only Kirill holds the key), Mel swore to Zuleeka that the party would honor their oath and return, leaving behind rations before Bianca guided them out through a hidden back entrance — a stone ring on the mountainside marked with old blood and gnawed bones, evidently used for challenges to pack leadership. The party fled across the mountain and made it back to Krezk by nightfall.

That night, Mel questioned Baron Dimitri Krezkov about the collars and, carefully, about the true story behind Ilya's transformation. Dimitri confirmed his estranged sister Zeleeka left Krezk decades ago because she embraced her lycanthropy rather than suppress it, and that she went on to marry the pack's former alpha — clarifying that Zuleeka herself never contacted or manipulated Ilya. The manipulation was the Abbott, who can shapeshift and had impersonated Zuleeka to convince Ilya his parents had lied to him about his nature, planting the doubt that led him to skip his cursed-blood suppressant and, eventually, kill his sister Kala. Mel chose not to reveal to Dimitri that his sister is alive and caged in the wolf den, intending to use the truth later as leverage to rally Krezk's aid against the pack.

The party earned 1,500 XP for striking the deal with Zuleeka. After a long rest, they set out the next morning for Argynvostholt, escorted partway by a revenant knight of the Order of the Silver Dragon who identified himself as bound by oath to guard the road. He directed them past the mansion's dragon statue and into the ruined foyer, then east through a dining hall to the Chapel of Mourning, warning them not to stray from that path or risk the mansion's other restless dead.

Within the chapel, bound in barbed black spectral wire between two pillars, they found Sir Godfrey Gwilym — a revenant of the Order, red-eyed and skeletal, who had summoned them through his knights. He revealed the Order's history: founded by Lord Argynvost, a silver dragon in disguise, the knights fought Strahd's invasion a decade after Argynvostholt's founding and were driven back to the mansion. In the last, hopeless battle, Commander Vladimir Horngaard led a final charge against Strahd himself and was slain; Godfrey fell to Strahd's second, a dusk elf with twin scimitars — Rahadin. Vladimir Horngaard's dying rage tore the knights back as revenants. They learned Strahd had desecrated Argynvost's remains, burning his bones and taking his skull as a trophy to Castle Ravenloft — an act that hardened Vladimir Horngaard's hatred into an eternal vow: rather than kill Strahd, he chose to let him rot as a miserable king of a dying, mist-locked land, and forbade the Order from striking at Strahd or his servants ever again. When a Barovian village rebellion (Doru's, three months past) demanded the knights intervene, Vladimir Horngaard refused and banished the entire Order in fury — all except Godfrey, whose own oath of loyalty still binds him to the mansion.

Godfrey explained that Argynvost's spirit, dormant for decades, has recently begun stirring again since Strahd's awakening — whispering of a "new generation of heroes." Believing the party might be those heroes, he sent his revenants to find them, but grew wary upon sensing the corruption of Flint's amber shard, suspecting them of being agents of the Amber Temple. After a failed persuasion attempt, he demanded Flint dispose of the shard in Lake Baratok before he'd help further; Vel intervened with a vow that Flint sought only knowledge, not power, and would not be allowed to fall further to the shard's corruption. Godfrey relented, though he still expects the shard discarded eventually, and he questioned Flint directly — Flint, partially transformed, confirmed he did not regret being kept alive by the pact.

Godfrey agreed to reveal the Sunsword's location only after the party completes "Vladimir Horngaard's final mission": exploring Argynvostholt and speaking with the spirits within to learn what they know of Argynvost's whispers. He warned them to avoid Commander Vladimir Horngaard's revenant guard, who patrols the upper halls, cannot be killed by any conventional means, and will show no mercy to intruders he suspects of seeking to move against Strahd — they must never speak Godfrey's name to him. Other hostile, maddened spirits relive their deaths endlessly and attack all outsiders on sight, but can be fooled into believing the party are fallen brothers-in-arms if they wear funeral vestments taken respectfully from a mass grave: south from Luna Lake, where the river meets the lake near an island, from an ancient stone slab walk two hundred paces west to find the resting place of nearly a thousand fallen soldiers of the Order. Godfrey taught them the old burial rite — "May the day's light last guide you home" — and warned that a pair of maddened giant spiders haunt the mansion's ballroom, dangerous enough that even the dead avoid them, and that no disguise will fool Vladimir Horngaard himself.

With the dinner at Castle Ravenloft looming the following night, the party made plans to seek out the burial ground and its vestments before continuing deeper into Argynvostholt.

Session 40: Entering the Wolf Den

The party's investigation led them to a lyre player named Bianca Stoyanovich, whose facade cracked under the influence of her own lyre music. Through Detect Thoughts, the group discovered her true identity as a werewolf married to Kiril, the pack's current alpha. They learned that Kiril had violently usurped leadership thirteen years ago from Emil Tornesku using mutated magic obtained from Baba Lysaga in the Berez Swamp, and that the pack now guards prisoners near Mother's Night Shrine.

While staking out the den, Zelly accidentally shot Mel. She then shared the story of her complicated past with Rudolph van Richten. After watching most werewolves leave the den to hunt, the party waited for the coast to clear and slipped inside. Along the way, they passed a supernatural pool of reflection that forced each member to confront eerie, unsettling visions of their true natures or fates.

Deep within the den, the party located Ilya and his aunt Zuleeka trapped in cages, suppressed by silver-spiked collars that sap their strength. While the former alpha, Skinnis, guards the area with a small contingent of wolves, Flint asked to take the Holy Symbol of Ravenkind from the shrine. Zuleeka warned them that stealing from Mother Night is lethal—as proven when Strahd's vampire guards previously tried to take it, only to freeze, die, and deteriorate.

Zuleeka can grant permission to take the artifact, but it comes at a steep price: the party must swear an oath to rescue her husband, Emil, from Strahd’s castle and help him regain power over the pack. This pact is bound by a strict deadline, as failing to fulfill the oath before the day following the upcoming New Moon on the 15th will cause the party's life strength to be sapped away.

Session 39: The Abbey and the Seance

Inside, the group met Zelly, an olive-skinned Vistani woman with a prosthetic leg. She revealed that she had returned to the Abbey to check on the Abbott, as she perceived he was becoming increasingly unstable. Zelly also noted that her friend, Van Richten, had returned to the area three months ago.

The group conducted a seance to contact spirits, during which they asked ten questions. The cards identified the spirit as Saint Markovia. When asked if the Abbott had made a deal with Strahd, the "Mist" card appeared, indicating a mysterious and unavoidable situation. The seance also revealed that to restore the Abbott, the party must obtain a specific physical artifact, with signs pointing toward the Amber Temple or the dungeons of Ravenloft as potential locations.

Following the seance, a surge of ethereal wind and a radiant burst of light filled the room, bringing visions of a white-robed woman, a winged man, and a silver statuette, accompanied by intense feelings of humility, hope, defiance, and sorrow. As the visions faded, Cloven, Otto, and Zigfric arrived at the door, expressing concern over having overheard the seance. The party engaged them, and Zigfric—who specifically knows about Sirus—was questioned on how to achieve "perfection". The trio asked us if we could ask Sirus some questions and in return they would proivide us with a secret treasure.

As the party departed the Abbey, they encountered the Abbott, who recognized and reclaimed the necklace Vel had taken from the Golem. The group proceeded toward their wagon to inventory their belongings with Zelly, with plans to travel to the Wolf Den to save Ilya.

Session 38: The Abbey and the Bride of Strahd

After a long rest, Flint distributed gifts to the party: a radiant weapon for Spigot, a mind sharpener for Mel, a sonic disruptor for Vel, and a recoil dampener for Ripley. The party also discovered a sketch of a woman behind a map, noting her resemblance to Dimitri. They learned that Esmerelda is in town staying at the abbey while helping search for the missing Ilya, who escaped on October 22. The party also learned of Zelecka, Dimitri's sister and a werewolf who has not been seen in Krezk for 30 years.

The party traveled to the Abbey of St. Markovia, observing children playing with genuine smiles despite the dark nature of the location. Upon arrival, they encountered stitched-together beings named Otto and Zigfric, assembled from spared parts. The Abbott, who had recently exploded a goat last week, greeted the party and obsessed over "perfection" while staring at a wall. They were brought inside by Cloven Belleview, a hunched patchwork figure with two heads.

The Abbott revealed that the abominations in the courtyard were once regular people. He claimed that Strahd is cursed and that he intends to become the ally Strahd needs. To break the curse on the land, the Abbott is training a being named Vasilka to be Strahd’s bride, utilizing Anna's heart to fill the void in Strahd's own heart. The Abbott requested that the party teach Vasilka about love, a task for which Vel volunteered. During this time, the party learned that a creature named Marzeena had escaped from her room, and that she and Sirus are siblings, with Sirus having been "promoted" to help Strahd. Do not tell the others that Sirus was promoted.

Session 37: Revelations in Krezk

Upon arriving in Krezk, the party encountered a dark-haired woman named Carly, who wielded a rapier and appeared to recognize them. They were also met by Zelly—potentially Esmerelda Davonic—who briefly checked her pockets before departing. The party was approached by the local Baron, Dimitri Krezkov, who seemed to be in charge of the village. During their introduction, they learned that a boy named Ilya was missing, and the Baron’s young daughter, Kala, was also present.

The party gained insight into the local history, including the story of Saint Markovia, who led a rebellion against Castle Ravenloft. They learned of the Abbott, a figure who arrived over a century ago without aging and is rumored to serve Strahd, with strange screams and laughter emanating from his Abbey. While staying at the Baron's cottage, the party assisted with chores, though Vel overheard the Baron attempting to convince his wife, Anna, to flee. Later, the Abbott visited the cottage without an invitation, displaying the ability to transform into an eagle upon his departure.

A tragic truth was revealed: after a werewolf attack left many wounded, Kala had died and Ilya had vanished. The Abbott offered to bring Kala back if Anna provided her heart to create a being named Vasilka, a request that left the parents distressed. Kala had returned, but she was not the same, and the parents had twelve days remaining to make their decision. Flint discovered Kala in her room, where she asked him to escort her to the basement that night to confront the monster that killed her.

In the basement, the party found claw marks, a lupine beast, and damaged silver shackles. They discovered that the Baron’s family line was cursed, with a werewolf bite in their history that manifested when children turned 13. The dark reality was that Ilya had been a werewolf, and after a failed potion, he had grown stronger and killed Kala. The party also investigated magical items, including a wand—a conduit to the fanes—and a green gemstone butterfly that had lost its magic.

Session 36: The Whispering Wall and Arrival in Krezk

Following the collapse of the tower, the party retrieved the staff despite being attacked by druids. Flint secured the staff while Spigot and Ripley focused on the green gem; Ripley cut open the statue to allow Spigot to retrieve it before it detonated from force damage. During the conflict, Ripley rescued Muriel from beneath the debris.

Strahd invited the party to the "Whispering Wall," which he described as the tomb of an ancient god known for offering visions to those who enter. Strahd recounted that he had entered the wall long ago, where he saw a white castle, a city, and heard church bells. Vel entered the wall, where the cold vanished and she found herself beneath the stars, recognizing a melody playing in the air. She encountered the "Ladies Three," who seemed to know her and indicated she was part of their stories. Before leaving through a manifesting archway, Vel was offered a grey and silver gemstone by Strahd; she chose the silver one, which is said to take the bearer to their desired destination. Strahd also revealed a dragon’s eye, a trinket that requires a spark of power to activate.

After returning to the group, the party confirmed that the three gems are tied to the "fanes". They reunited with Davian at the winery, where it was confirmed that the Martikov family are wereravens and spies. Davian provided Spigot with an obsidian whistle, and the party learned of a need to find a token of the Ladies—specifically a "handless spear" buried in the Eastern mountains at the Pillar Stone of Ravenloft.

Following a short rest and a level-up, the party departed for Krezk. Along the road, they encountered a figure in rusty armor and were warned that werewolves have become increasingly bold. Upon arriving at Krezk, the group gained entry by leveraging their wine delivery; once inside, Ripley felt a pull from his scarf, and a mysterious voice called out to the group, suggesting they move closer to share what they were carrying.

The party also noted that Muriel overheard Strahd’s earlier reading, which mentioned "The Dark lord, Master of shadows" and a "Maiden with the raven hair". Additionally, the reading spoke of an innocent on the way to the foden and that the "Wall of whispers await your tribute".

Session 35: Strahd Appears

Muriel provided a map to Yester Hill, where the party observed bright lightning flashes on the boulders and heard distant drumming. Upon their arrival, they found Strahd waiting atop the hill on his nightmare horse; upon seeing Flint, Strahd remarked that the Amber Temple had taken notice of him. Strahd offered the party a reward if they could stop the ritual without his interference, instructing them to meet him at the base of the whispering wall to collect it. Below, thirteen cloaked figures, led by a dark-skinned woman, stood in a circle. Strahd introduced the party to Ludmilla, one of his vampire brides who had been watching the ravens. During her monologue, Ludmilla confirmed that Muriel is a were-raven, noting that Elric had also been a were-raven; when a raven previously captured by Strahd died, it left behind a human corpse. Additionally, Ludmilla stated that Baba Lasaga has sworn to destroy all the Martikovs. Regarding Flint's condition, he is currently unable to summon his cannon or perform magic. He received a message from Seriac, referred to as the "Master of the Hunter," which stated: "Seek out the mountain, find the temple — let's hunt a vampire". After Strahd departed to allow the party to interfere with the ritual, they engaged in battle against Ludmilla and the druids, ultimately stopping the ritual before Ludmilla fled.

Session 34: Keepers of the Feather Revealed

Following pressure from Stefania, Davian revealed that the Martikov family are the Keepers of the Feather, a group he leads. This is a group that opposes Strahd and gather intelligence on him. As they entered the village proper, the party realized with heavy hearts that they had been transported to the year 735, trapped within the borders of a dark and unfamiliar domain.

Session 3: The Fall of the Dursts

The final confrontation within the Durst House reached a violent conclusion as Flint struck down Walter, finally bringing an end to the abomination’s tormented existence. The victory was immediate but short-lived; as the source of the house's corruption was severed, the structure began a rapid, violent collapse around the party. They managed a narrow escape, scrambling out of the wreckage just as the walls gave way. However, the safety they expected to find outside was a cruel illusion. As they stepped away from the ruins, they were instantly enveloped by the thick, encroaching Mists of Barovia. Unbeknownst to the party, their escape from the house had only served to deliver them directly into the heart of Barovia, Strahd’s domain.

Session 2: The Durst Mansion

The party’s investigation into the Durst House quickly descended into a nightmare as they navigated the mansion’s labyrinthine secrets, uncovering hidden chambers and defiled shrines that exposed the full, grotesque extent of the family’s corruption. Walter's crib had the inscription of "He is the Ancient. He is the Land" (A reference to Strahd). Amidst these grim discoveries, the party secured an amber amulet key, a pivotal artifact that seems inextricably linked to the house’s dark power. The tension peaked when the group came face-to-face with the pulsating, abominable form of Walter.

Session 1: Daggerford & the Orphanage

The party’s shared roots in the Daggerford orphanage were shaken when Grigor One-Eye revealed that a mysterious, cloaked stranger had been inquiring about them, brandishing unsettlingly specific knowledge of their past. He mentioned Zelly, a companion they had grown up alongside, was potentially the true child of their foster mother. This shocking discovery fundamentally alters the group's understanding of their upbringing and leaves them questioning the true nature of the bonds they forged in the orphanage. This prompted a return to the site of their youth, where they discovered that their childhood was inextricably linked to the dark history of the Durst family. Among the haunting remnants of that time—including children clutching stuffed toy wolves—they uncovered ties to Gustav, Elizabeth, and the siblings Thorn and Rose, while also learning that their former nanny, Klara (“Lady Watcher”), may have been the mother of the ill-fated Walter.
