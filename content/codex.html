The Codex of Barovia

A reference shelf, not a story log — everything grouped by the mystery it belongs to, so you can look a thing up without hunting through session notes.

Strahd & the Von Zarovich Tragedy

The founding wound of Barovia — everything traces back to one wedding day.

Strahd von Zarovich

The vampire lord and absolute master of Barovia. Once a great warrior-king who conquered this valley for his father Barov; years of war hollowed him out long before he became undead. On his brother Sergei's wedding day, Strahd murdered him and sealed a pact with Death in his own blood after Sergei's betrothed, Tatyana, chose Sergei over him. Castle guards (led by Leo Delinzia) fired arrows that pierced him to the soul but didn't kill him — he rose as "Vampyr" instead.

Sleeps far below Castle Ravenloft among the dead; will retreat to the castle's heights only once the party comes for him in earnest (Tarokka Card 5). His one true fear is the Sunsword — "I fear and hate it as much as the sun." Direct encounters so far have all been him toying with the party rather than fighting: he personally killed Flint at Yester Hill (Session 34) as punishment for Spiggot's defiance, then offered the Amber Pact. He extended a formal dinner invitation via Rahadin, now imminent as of Session 45.

Sergei & the Sunsword

Strahd's younger brother — handsome, beloved, and everything Strahd no longer was. Sergei brought the Sunsword to Barovia as a wedding gift: a blade with a platinum hilt and a deep blue sapphire, igniting into pure sunlight. Strahd murdered him rather than lose Tatyana to him. The sword itself is the one weapon Strahd is known to fear; he tried to dispose of it but apparently failed. Hidden in "an amber prison, where the devil fears to tread" (Tarokka Card 3) — almost certainly the Amber Temple, reachable only after Argynvostholt ("the House of the Fallen Dragon").

Tatyana & Ireena — The Eternal Hunt

Tatyana threw herself from Castle Ravenloft's walls in despair rather than face Strahd after Sergei's murder; no trace was ever found. Strahd has hunted her reincarnations across the centuries since. Strong evidence points to Ireena being Tatyana reborn: the same red hair, an unexplained childhood appearance in the Slavich Woods, recognition of Lake Zarovich from a dream, Strahd's open claim on her, and his minions never directly attacking her. A note found in a hidden cache confirmed she was raised believing her name was "Tatianna" by adoptive parents who wished her a path toward light.

Rahadin, the Chamberlain

Strahd's dusk-elf chamberlain and enforcer, wielding twin scimitars. Delivers Strahd's messages and ultimatums in person (the assault ultimatum at Barovia village, the formal dinner invitation). Confirmed via Sir Godfrey Gwilym's account to be the one who killed Godfrey in the Order's final battle centuries ago — meaning Rahadin has served Strahd since at least the fall of Argynvostholt.

The Order of the Silver Dragon & Argynvostholt

A century-old grief that's still actively shaping the mansion today. The party's current main thread (Sessions 41–45).

Lord Argynvost

Founder of the Order of the Silver Dragon — secretly a silver dragon in disguise, known only to his most trusted knights. Died fighting Strahd's invasion in his true dragon form rather than surrender, per his own recovered final testament ("throw off this guise... let it spark terror in their hearts"). Strahd desecrated his remains afterward: burned his bones, took his skull to Castle Ravenloft as a trophy. His spirit lingered for decades urging Godfrey away from vengeance, went dormant, and has recently begun stirring again — too weak to manifest, but whispering of "a new generation of heroes" through the mansion's other ghosts.

Open thread: A tower gargoyle's prophecy (Session 45) says the beacon can't be lit until his remains rest again in his mausoleum at Argynvostholt — remains the party believes are the "hall of bones" trophy room Van Richten described inside Castle Ravenloft.

Sir Godfrey Gwilym

A revenant knight bound in barbed spectral wire in Argynvostholt's Chapel of Mourning. Fell to Rahadin in the Order's last battle. Kept undead by his own oath of loyalty to Vladimir and the Order — the same oath that forbids him from ever striking at Strahd or his servants. Summoned the party, suspecting they're the heroes Argynvost's spirit has whispered of; wary of Flint's amber corruption until Vel vowed to keep it in check. Will reveal the Sunsword's location once "Vladimir's final mission" is complete. A torn portrait found in Session 44 — two men clasping hands, faces worn away, builds matching Godfrey and Vladimir — suggests the two silver vow rings found in the wine cellar are theirs, hinting at a private bond neither the party nor Godfrey has openly acknowledged.

Commander Vladimir Horngaard

Former commander of the Order, slain by Strahd himself in the final battle. His dying rage dragged his knights back as revenants. Rather than let anyone kill Strahd, he vowed Strahd should suffer eternally as a miserable king of a dying land — and forbade the Order from ever striking at Strahd or his servants again. When his knights demanded to break that vow during Doru's rebellion, he banished the entire Order except Godfrey.

Met face-to-face in Session 45: revealed as a skeletal, red-eyed revenant beneath rusted armor. Flatly refused to release his oath and turned hostile once pressed, forcing the party to flee. Let slip that "if Argynvost can't be heard, no one can" — dismissing hope of the dragon's return, though whether that's true or manipulation is unconfirmed. Cannot be fooled by any disguise and is effectively unkillable by conventional means.

Zorya, the Pseudodragon Companion

Lord Argynvost's own pseudodragon companion "from better days, before the fog." Trapped as a small, mute, silvery wisp somewhere in Argynvostholt until the party freed her in Sessions 42–43. Understands Common and Draconic but cannot speak — communicates by leading the party to hidden clues and objects. Grew visibly sorrowful when asked where Argynvost is now. Lit the tower's silver braziers on Vel's request in Session 45, triggering the gargoyle's prophecy. Brother Marek's clue ("the younger of the twin dragons") almost certainly refers to her.

Brother Marek & the Icon of Dawn's Grace

A chaplain of the Order, killed defending Argynvostholt's third floor in the final battle. Summoned from the Ethereal Plane in Session 44 after his prayer book was recovered (from a spider-infested ballroom) and read aloud. Bitter and hopeless until Mel matched his despair with genuine honesty. Named the vision's silver statuette the "Icon of Dawn's Grace" — a symbolic vessel for a shard of divinity — and gave the cryptic clue: "seek out the younger of the twin dragons above, reignite the silver flame it once wielded, reminded of the oath it once served." Also noted the Sunsword was forged for a King Barov's line, passed to a younger son, and rumored hidden in "the house of the far."

The Vow Rings & Andral's Letter

Two matching silver rings, inscribed "From this until the end of days" (the same line closing the Order's full oath), found hidden in an empty wine barrel. Originally suspected to belong to Argynvost and a letter-writer named Andral, who separately begged Argynvost to make peace with Strahd rather than fight — a plea Argynvost rejected. A torn portrait found later (Session 44) suggests the rings actually belong to Godfrey and Vladimir instead, leaving Andral's letter a separate, unrelated historical thread about Argynvost's politics rather than his heart. Neither mystery is fully resolved.

The Gargoyle's Prophecy

Verse recited by Argynvostholt's tower gargoyle after Zorya lit its silver braziers (Session 45): "When the dragon dreams its dream, within its rightful tomb, the light of Argynvost will beam and rid this land of gloom." Read by the party as confirmation that the beacon (and likely the means to break Vladimir's oath, or claim the Icon of Dawn's Grace) requires Argynvost's actual remains to be returned to his mausoleum — remains the party suspects are kept as a trophy inside Castle Ravenloft.

The Amber Temple & the Amber Shards

Flint's slow-burning corruption arc, and the destination it's all pointing toward.

The Amber Temple

A cursed temple in the mountains, predating Strahd and Barovia itself, where he made his original pact with the Dark Powers. Almost certainly holds the Sunsword. Reachable only through Argynvostholt. Van Richten warned the party away from it in Session 45 without offering further insight — he considers it dangerous even by his standards.

Flint's Amber Shard — Norganas & Seriac

Flint carries a fused pair of amber shards. The entity within goes by Norganas ("Finger of Oblivion," from his mother's shard) and Seriac ("Master of the Hunter," from Isek's shard, which Flint chose after Strahd killed him at Yester Hill). All Amber Shards share the same Abjuration properties and trace back to the Amber Temple. Costs so far: black blood, a left arm turning red with barbed spires, no heartbeat or breath. Its most recent offer — "Desperate Hunger" — wants Flint to become a predator.

Flint's Soul-Trap Device

A new and troubling development. During Session 44's spider fight, Flint began perceiving spirits trapped by Argynvostholt's "closed system" — his shard entity told him "the dead have nowhere to go." In Session 45, he built a working prototype: an amber-sealed iron vault triggered by a foot pedal, capable of capturing a dying creature's soul in a burst of violet light. Tested extensively (and grimly) on rats and mice. He hasn't decided whether to use it offensively, defensively, as a rechargeable "battery," or some hybrid — and hasn't told the rest of the party what it actually does.

The Ladies of the Fanes & the Land's Curse

Why the land itself obeys Strahd — and the oldest quest thread in the campaign.

The Ladies Three & the Three-Pointed Star

Three ancient goddesses (the Rozana) worshipped in Barovia before Strahd's arrival, symbolized by a three-pointed star: the Weaver (magic and creation), the Seeker (ravens and knowledge), and the Huntress (wolves and the wild). Strahd claimed all three Fanes and bent their servants to his will. The Keepers of the Feather likely descend from the Seeker's servants; the werewolf/Mother Night connection near Lake Baratok relates to the Huntress.

The Three Fanes

Sacred sites predating Strahd, each once holding a gemstone blessed by the Weaver: the Swamp Fane (Ruins of Berez area), the Forest Fane (Yester Hill area), and the Mountain Fane (Tsolenka Pass area, unconfirmed). Strahd's Tome confirms he drew power from all three — "thus I have become the Land." Restoring them could weaken his hold on Barovia. Three winery gemstones (stolen from the Martikovs) are actually Gems of the Fanes, meant to be carried to Soldav in the Balinoks — the First Folk's hidden refuge — to restore the Ladies' power.

Kavan & the Betrayal of the First Folk

Spirit of an ancient chieftain of the First Folk, met in the Slavich Woods. Centuries ago he broke his people's laws, stole the Huntress's sacred spear from the Forest Fane, and waged war using its power — until the wereravens (servants of the Seeker) hid the Fanes' gems to stop him, and his own people exiled him. His tribe later split: those who opposed Strahd fled to Soldav (now the Mountain Folk); those who submitted stayed in the Svalich Wood (now the Forest Folk). Soldav lies hidden by enchanted mist somewhere in the Balinoks, marked by "a place where two waterfalls meet," and requires a token of the Ladies to find or enter.

The Keepers of the Feather

A secret were-raven network opposing Strahd, led by Davian Martikov. Confirmed members: Davian, Erwin, Danika, Stefania, Muriel, and the late Elric Martikov, plus Van Richten as an ally. Their economic base is the Wizards of Wine Winery — its magical grape-growing was powered by three enchanted gemstones (the Fane gems above), now stolen by a raven, Baba Lysaga's scarecrows, and the Forest Folk respectively. Baba Lysaga has sworn to destroy the entire family.

Mother Night & the Wolf Den

A resolved artifact quest that opened a brand new, time-limited one.

The Holy Symbol of Ravenkind

Tarokka Card 2. A sun pendant with a large ruby, once belonging to Mother Night, the dark deity of lycanthropes. Retrieved in Session 41 from her shrine in the Wolf Den, hills above Lake Baratok. Mel now carries and is attuned to it — his eyes briefly burned yellow when he took it. Stealing it outright is lethal (Strahd's own vampire guards froze, died, and deteriorated attempting it); the party instead received it as a gift, bound by oath.

Zuleeka, Emil, and the Pack

Zuleeka Tornesku — Dimitri Krezkov's estranged sister, secretly alive and caged in the Wolf Den, married to the pack's deposed alpha Emil Tornesku. Kirill seized pack leadership 13 years ago using magic supplied by Baba Lysaga, and now rules through his wife Bianca Stoyanovich and the enforcer Skinnis. Zuleeka granted the Holy Symbol in exchange for the party's oath to free Emil from Castle Ravenloft and restore him as alpha. Dimitri doesn't yet know his sister is alive — Mel is holding that fact in reserve as leverage.

The Oath's Deadline

If the party fails to rescue Emil and restore him as alpha before the day following the New Moon on the 15th of Neyavr, Mother Night's curse triggers: maximum hit points reduced by one-seventh, and a cumulative -1 to all stats every day thereafter, until the oath is fulfilled or the party dies. Zuleeka or another cleric of Mother Night could potentially release the party from the oath early.

The Abbott's Bride & the Krezk Curse

A quieter horror running in parallel to Argynvostholt — currently on pause, not resolved.

The Krezkov Family Curse

A hereditary werewolf-bite curse in the Krezkov bloodline, manifesting after a child turns 13. Baron Dimitri's son Ilya succumbed to it and, after a failed cure attempt, killed his own sister Kala. Anna Krezkov (Dimitri's wife) was offered a bargain by the Abbott: her own heart, in exchange for Kala's resurrection.

The Abbott & Vasilka

An ageless, shapeshifting figure (can become an eagle) who arrived at the Abbey of St. Markovia over a century ago. Obsessed with "perfection," he creates abominations from human remains and believes Strahd is cursed rather than evil. He's building Vasilka, a construct meant to become Strahd's bride and — using Anna's heart — supposedly break the curse on the land. The party has been enlisted to teach Vasilka about love. His experiments include Cloven Belleview, Otto, and Zigfric.

Saint Markovia & the Stained-Glass Vision

A legendary figure who once led a rebellion against Castle Ravenloft, honored by the Abbey that bears her name. A seance at the Abbey (Session 39) contacted her spirit directly, revealing that restoring the Abbott requires a physical artifact from the Amber Temple or Ravenloft's dungeons. A stained-glass gallery discovered in Argynvostholt (Session 43) depicts an angel resembling the Abbott bestowing a small silver statuette on her — later named by Brother Marek as the Icon of Dawn's Grace — while an older man watches with an expression the party read as envious rather than reverent.

Baba Lysaga & the Berez Swamp

A recurring threat the party has only ever seen the consequences of, never the witch herself.

Baba Lysaga

An ancient, powerful witch based at the Ruins of Berez in the southern swamps. Created the flying blight creatures that attacked the winery, stole the Red Dragon Crush gemstone via scarecrows, and has sworn to destroy the entire Martikov family. Supplied Kirill with the magic he used to seize the Wolf Den 13 years ago. The party has never confronted her directly — every encounter with her influence has been indirect.

The Swamp Witch & the Huntress's Elk

In Session 42, a woman in mud-spattered robes riding an undead dire wolf harvested the heart and liver from a dying, Huntress-marked elk near Luna Lake — likely Baba Lysaga, though never confirmed face-to-face. A captured ghoul separately described a "Dark Master," a "circle of stones" that shakes when a "swamp woman" visits, and "a great wave from the mountains into a flow" — an unsolved lead possibly pointing to a Fane ritual site.

The Vistani & Madam Eva's Tarokka Reading

The map the whole campaign has been drawn from — and the counter-map Strahd handed the party in response.

The Five-Card Reading

Delivered by Madam Eva at the Tser Pool Encampment (Session 10): Card 1 (History) → the Sacred Tome of Strahd, resolved via Leo Delinzia. Card 2 (Holy Symbol) → the Holy Symbol of Ravenkind, resolved via the Wolf Den. Card 3 (Sunsword) → hidden in an "amber prison," in progress via Argynvostholt. Card 4 (Ally) → a Vistani wanderer at Saint Markovia's Abbey, presumed to be Zelly, unconfirmed. Card 5 (Enemy's Location) → Castle Ravenloft's heights, not yet relevant.

Strahd's Counter-Reading

Overheard by Vel in a trance the same day as the real reading: "The Sword lies in the hand of the Dead / The Ally walks in the Shadow / Cast a light on the Traitor and call him friend / They will trust the wrong soul, let them chase a ghost." Read as Strahd mocking or subverting Madam Eva's reading — possibly true, possibly a deliberate destabilization tactic, possibly both. No specific traitor has been identified yet; the party has been advised to treat every ally with some caution.

The Zelly / Esmeralda Question

Unresolved inconsistency worth clarifying: the wiki currently treats "Zelly" and "Esmeralda D'Avenir" as the same person in one NPC entry, but also has a separate entry for an "Ezmeralda D'Avenir" met independently at Kazan's Tower as Rictavio/Van Richten's student. The party has never explicitly confirmed on-screen whether these are one person or two. Since Card 4 of the Tarokka reading hinges on this identity, it's worth settling before it becomes load-bearing.

Prophecies & Verses — Quick Reference

Every verse the party has heard, in one place, for whenever someone says "wait, how did that one go?"

Arabelle's Prophecy (Session 22)

"In darkest night, the light shall flee, no dawn to break, with no reprieve. From the grave, the dead shall climb, their restless march a baleful sign. At castle's peak, a heart beats red, its hunger deep and ever-fed. Should silence fall, the skies shall crack, a thousand souls in torment black." Spoken through Arabelle in an older voice resembling Madam Eva's, right after her rescue.

Strahd's Counter-Reading (Session 9)

"She thinks the cards speak truth, that rivers can be dammed / The Sword lies in the hand of the Dead / The Ally walks in the Shadow / Cast a light on the Traitor and call him friend / They will trust the wrong soul, let them chase a ghost." Overheard by Vel in a trance after Strahd's roadside ambush.

The Order's Oath

Full text found inscribed on a silver tablet in Argynvostholt's vault, and again carved into the war room's round table: "I solemnly vow to uphold the virtues of the Order of the Silver Dragon, with honor as my shield and truth as my guide... So do I swear in the name of the silver dragon, from this until the end of days." The closing line also appears on the two mystery vow rings.

The Gargoyle's Prophecy (Session 45)

"When the dragon dreams its dream, within its rightful tomb, the light of Argynvost will beam and rid this land of gloom." Hissed by Argynvostholt's tower gargoyle after Zorya lit its silver braziers.

Argynvost's Final Testament (found Session 43)

Closing lines: "I have laid down my trappings beneath the tower's watchful gaze, so that others may carry my legacy in my stead. Though my body will die, my spirit will live on where the oath and fire of the silver dragon survive. Let it serve as a beacon of light against the darkness." Signed simply "A."

Kavan's Directions to Soldav

"I recall only the landmark that marks its entrance: a place where two waterfalls meet as one... without a token of the Ladies, you will never find the entrance — and the elders of Soldav will never open their gates to you." Soldav lies somewhere in the Balinok Mountains, veiled by enchanted, memory-erasing mist.
